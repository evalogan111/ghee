Golden Farm has supplied pure butter ghee across Australia since 2009, and the wholesale side is the larger half of the business: pails to the kitchens, bakeries and distributors that go through ghee faster than any jar could keep up with. This page covers who buys by the pail, what the sizes suit, and how an account works.

Pricing for the trade

Wholesale pricing works on price per kilogram, which is why the pails are the better value for a working kitchen: the 10kg pail lands at a lower rate per kilo than the jars. Exact trade pricing depends on the sizes and volumes you take, so it is confirmed on enquiry rather than published as a single figure. What stays fixed is the product. The ghee in a wholesale pail is the same pure butter ghee that goes into the retail jars, so there is no quality trade-off in buying at volume.

Delivery and pickup from Sydney

Everything ships from the warehouse in Prestons, in south-west Sydney. Sydney kitchens can collect or arrange delivery; trade orders outside Sydney are freighted. The practical advantage of a single Sydney source is consistency of supply: one warehouse, one product, one point of contact, rather than a pallet that passes through several hands before it reaches you. For a kitchen, that is the difference between a staple you plan around and one you chase.

Beyond ghee

Ghee is the core of the wholesale range, but it is not the only line that moves through the same warehouse and the same trade accounts. Kitchens and grocers that buy ghee by the pail also take cooking oils, basmati rice, lentils, spices and other pantry staples on the same order, which keeps a single supplier relationship doing more work. If you are setting up a trade account, it is worth asking what else you can consolidate onto it.

Why buy direct rather than through a middle layer

A lot of wholesale ghee reaches kitchens through two or three hands: an importer, a distributor, then a cash-and-carry. Each hand adds cost and distance, and the further a product travels from its source the harder consistency is to guarantee. Buying direct from the source that packs the ghee cuts that chain down to one relationship. For a kitchen that means a clearer price, a single point of contact when something needs sorting, and the same product every time rather than whatever the distributor happened to have that week. It is the same logic that makes a direct supplier worth setting up even when a general wholesaler is more convenient in the short term.
